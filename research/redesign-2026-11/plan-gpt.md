Give Options the full work area, with a short preview above its aligned rows and a persistent action area on the right. Keep the big canvas on spatial steps. [Visual feedback](feedback.md#L7) · [Options](feedback.md#L177) · [Findings](feedback.md#L203)

Claude batches design edits but applies slicer edits instantly without another check. A walls, support or fan edit can invalidate strength, weight or cooling results. [Fixes and confirmation](feedback.md#L211) · [Overrides and findings](feedback.md#L230) · [Focused recheck](feedback.md#L305)

Offer one route, with Complete controlled by what the user allows the app to change. [One flow](feedback.md#L194) · [Analysis depth](feedback.md#L266) · [Printer management](feedback.md#L69)
- **Keep Quality complete as a route.** Claude correctly omits strength, but postpones implementing Quality until after the full structural launch. Implement the route, its distinct candidate ranking and its “no load entered” contract alongside the structural route. Unlock a selectable Quality card with an end-to-end path and honest surface-quality estimates. Then add research-backed tuning, calibrations and thermal detail as they become usable. ## Technical risks and a better order

- **The current orientation API can overlook the strongest pose.** It makes a printability shortlist before applying load scores. The cheap strength sweep uses one isotropic solve per load case/configuration to score a set of directions. An anisotropic solve checks the final print direction and settings. Start the new search from the broader candidate generator, preserve strength and printability finalists, and check the finalists at their actual settings. Similar height and footprint alone cannot establish equivalence under load. The real search is finite, so promise “best options we found.” [Owner on orientation and trade-offs](feedback.md#L142) · [Orientation API](../../core/fdmcore/api.py#L428) · [Fast and full strength solves](../../core/fdmcore/strength.py#L516)
- **A goal floor is a filter on an estimate.** The transcript gives example values of ×5, ×3 and ×1.5 and says “it will never break.” Real parts can break. Use “Estimated margin ×3.4 · target ×3,” retain the existing continuous-load safeguard, and report unsupported load conditions explicitly. Avoid touching legacy thresholds by swapping out the fixed-orientation optimizer's `OPT_TARGET`. Add a new policy for the new workflow. Thicker layers can change speed, effective skin thickness and bonding, with the direction of a strength change depending on the setup. Use model and calibration evidence, not a universal thicker-is-stronger rule. [Floor examples](feedback.md#L146) · [Layer and nozzle feedback](feedback.md#L253) · [Existing service policy](../../core/fdmcore/service.py#L20) · [Existing optimizer](../../core/fdmcore/api.py#L905)
- **The browser already estimates support grams, but its approximation has limits.** Reuse the sliced support-volume estimate with the candidate's density for finalists. It uses an approximate fill fraction, and is a poor substitute for a configured slicer's toolpath where support style or support material differs. Show estimated support grams as a separate value. Similarly, the solver accepts face regions and spot patches. An edge-wide load or hold needs an explicit edge-to-patch mapping and resolution checks. Claude's Alt-click point load and kg unit add complexity beyond the transcript's N and MPa workflow. [Support feedback](feedback.md#L151) · [Force feedback](feedback.md#L116) · [Support estimate](../../core/fdmcore/api.py#L697) · [Load preparation](../../core/fdmcore/strength.py#L159)
- **Complete analysis requires a geometry and export pipeline.** `placement.py` fits and positions the build pose, while `additions.py` produces particular edit shapes. Neither automatically searches stress-guided model changes. Use stress zones and existing modifier exports first. Geometry variants need browser manifold booleans, checked load remapping, and reslicing and strength validation. A hidden cavity only earns a benefit if it creates printable perimeter paths in the target slicer. Combined valid edits can still pinch a mesh, as T-490 illustrates. [Complete analysis](feedback.md#L266) · [Variant solver](../../core/fdmcore/api.py#L1887) · [Combined edits](../../TASKS.md#L956)
- **Print assessment and tuned files have different dependencies.** Basic heat/cooling and warping estimates work without OrcaSlicer in the browser. The detailed thermal model and its validation are pending. T-481 is an opt-in feed-rate and fan rewrite to compatible G-code. Temperature changes belong in slicer settings followed by a new slice. T-477 is a go/no-go for T-480 onward, including the tuning path. A browser-generated G-code download also depends on T-127. [Owner on simulation and export](feedback.md#L293) · [Thermal tasks](../../TASKS.md#L818)
- **Build a vertical slice before cutting over.** Bring T-491's header fix into the new shell when it is available. Prove one desktop loop from a sample through comparison and export, with saved projects and back navigation intact. In parallel within the implementation schedule, validate the 3D selection and browser optimizer costs before committing to all the screens. Exercise the flow with realistic long labels and an actual loaded mesh. Retire the old app entry and phone layout when the replacement covers the agreed launch scope. Use design reviews where they resolve a choice, especially spatial interaction and four-column comparison. [Desktop and visual feedback](feedback.md#L7) · [Claimed header work](../../TASKS.md#L962)

# Redesign plan

## Desktop shell and interaction system

- **Start at 1280 × 720 CSS pixels.** At that size use a 56 px header, a 40 px compact progress strip on workflow screens, 24 px outer gutters and a 16 px gap between the canvas and a 352 px side panel. Give the canvas the remaining width, roughly 864 px. At 1600 px let the canvas grow and cap the panel at 400 px. Keep the header in an explicit first row of the app root. Scroll inside the panel with its title and 64 px action footer fixed. At narrower desktop widths or high zoom, allow a desktop workspace to scroll and keep the header and advance action reachable. Respect zoom, reduced motion and forced colors. Remove app phone layouts, competing mobile navigation and the obsolete phone promises in PRODUCT.md.
- **Use the space each task needs.** Start and Part are centered layouts. Holds, Loads, Simulation and Problems use a canvas with a right panel. Options replaces that split with a full-width comparison. Settings has its own navigation. The progress strip has five groups, “Part,” “Setup,” “Use,” “Options” and “Print.” Show the specific step in the workspace title. Back and Next stay in the bottom-right action area. Completed steps are revisitable. Going back preserves inputs, camera and scroll where practical. Advancing selects the next valid step for the goal. Remove the mode switch and disable Expert. If levels return, give Advanced and Expert the same step structure.
- **Use a small set of controls.** Use 4/8/12/16/24/32/48 px spacing, 15–16 px body text, 13 px secondary metadata and 24–28 px step titles. Use neutral off-white panels, dark readable type, a muted matte 3D stage and one blue accent for actions. Reserve amber and red for risk or errors. Pair color with words, symbols or patterns. Style semantic buttons, radios, checkboxes and inputs consistently. Give hit targets at least 36 × 36 px, primary controls 44 px height, and every component a visible focus ring. Use one primary action, a short heading and only the hint needed to make the next choice. Put explanation, evidence and limitations in More. Use inline errors at the affected field, a persistent error for failed jobs and a brief status message for completed actions.
- **Make the canvas usable from the keyboard.** Tab enters and leaves the canvas as a single stop. Once focused, the user can open a searchable feature list with thumbnail/labels and a 3D highlight on focus. Arrow keys move within a menu or feature group, Enter selects, and Escape closes the topmost menu or cancels the current gesture and returns focus. Provide keyboard orbit and zoom from the focused canvas and labeled Reset view and Section view actions. Avoid global traps and single-key shortcuts while editing a field. Move focus to each newly entered heading, restore focus when closing a dialog or returning from Settings, and announce load completion and material status changes through a polite live region.
- **Give every job and result a revision.** Store the source part, current committed model, optional preview, printer/profile snapshot, exact options and kept choices, plus result revisions. Mark stale results when their inputs change. Cache reusable calculations by dependency. Settings changes affect a running project when selected or explicitly refreshed. Handle Back or file replacement during a task by canceling or ignoring the obsolete result. Run one numerical worker with bounded memory. Report stage and elapsed time, show an estimate range only where measured, offer Cancel, and keep the last valid result available. On failure or worker restart, reload the last committed model and settings from the main thread. Export always refers to a named snapshot.

## 2. Setup and Environment

**Setup.** Show the imported part, name and dimensions with **Replace part** in the stage toolbar. The right panel has three groups.

1. **What do you have?** A searchable Printer picker with recent and custom presets, then Material and Nozzle as a paired row when labels fit. Both start at **We'll determine this later**. An explicitly selected material or nozzle becomes a hard constraint, with a visible clear action. Use “Nozzle to fit” in results if the suggested diameter differs from the one installed. **Manage printers and materials** opens the corresponding Settings tab and returns here with the part and choices intact. Require a printer to continue. When a profile has no reliable nozzle capability list, use its known diameter and allow the user to confirm other supported sizes.
2. **What matters most?** Five equal radio cards in a two-column group, with the fifth spanning or aligned consistently. Balanced, Strong, Fast, Light, Quality. Default Balanced. Show one line under the selection, for example “Use less plastic, including support.” Selecting Quality after entering loads preserves their definitions in the project and skips their use. Selecting a structural goal again restores those inputs.
3. **What can you change?** Slicer settings, Design, Both. Default Slicer settings. Both permits combinations. Slicer settings permits orientation/settings search and keeps design suggestions available for deliberate later override. Design means search geometry against a held print recipe. Use the imported recipe when available, or present printer defaults for the user to confirm. Keep adjustable material/nozzle visible as hardware and material decisions until a baseline is confirmed, then show what is fixed. Use a proposed default pose, check it, and allow choosing a baseline pose in analysis when needed. Fast and Extended can assess the baseline in this mode. Complete can try geometry variants. If the user needs search across both geometry and print settings, they can switch to Both.

**Environment.** In the right panel offer familiar temperature situations such as Indoors, Outdoors, Hot car and Custom. Show the actual maximum expected use temperature in °C so it can be corrected. Offer everyday exposure chips for Water or moisture, Food or drink, Sun and rain, Dirt or scraping, Workshop oils or cleaners, and None. None clears other selections and Unknown leaves suitability unverified. Offer an optional detail for a known solvent or oil without making the user research chemicals to proceed. Tag use temperature separately from the room, enclosure or bed temperature during printing. Reuse existing heat, UV, abrasive and enclosure screening. Add sourced data on specific exposure where available. Food contact gets a process and material suitability note with the limits of the data. Unknown contact resistance remains unknown. The optimizer can recommend another material or report “We couldn't verify a material for this use.” Next goes to Holds or, for Quality, Analysis.

## 3. Holds, Loads and Load details

**Selection in 3D.** Draw a soft fill and crisp outline for the hovered face, or a brighter edge stroke near a real feature edge. Use a starting hit tolerance of 6 CSS px projected to screen. Pick visible edges including inside corners, with a Section view to expose occluded surfaces. STEP uses source face IDs where available. Mesh imports use segmented faces and feature polylines, with a clear “Select a patch” fallback where a whole face is ambiguous. Reuse existing region data and add edge data explicitly. Show a short target label, for example “Inside edge,” before committing. A click without a drag opens a cursor-centered radial menu. Keep the sectors within the viewport, with a compact attached menu when corner clearance is insufficient. Orbit and zoom remain distinct from selecting. Track the anchor in part coordinates so marker positions survive camera and print-pose changes.

**Holds.** A click presents **Hold this face**, **Hold this edge** if applicable and **Cancel**. Hovering a sector updates the footprint to be fixed. Selecting it shows the confirmed footprint and a lock marker. For an edge, show the finite band that will be held. Represent the band by sampled points in part coordinates and use voxel-surface mapping with a measured radius. Validate aggregated mapping above the analysis grid limit and surface errors before the full job where possible. Multiple holds work, with select, edit and remove in the right-hand list. The same actions are available from the marker menu. If holds cover a large share of the surface or overlap a load, show contextual guidance such as “Only hold the areas that are fixed in real life.” After one valid hold show **Next** and allow more. Quality skips this screen.

**Loads.** Hover and click an exposed target, then choose **Add load here** from the radial menu. Place a direction gizmo at the anchor. Offer push/pull along the normal for a planar face and ±X/±Y/±Z relative to the original model. Identify the coordinate frame with a triad. Ring handles rotate the vector with 15° snapping and Shift for free drag. Keyboard users can pick an axis or normal, focus a ring and nudge the vector with arrows, with a smaller increment on Shift. Use arrows and directional text as feedback while adjusting.

After direction, open a small anchored entry with one number, **N | MPa**, **Set load** and **Cancel**. N is a total force. MPa is an input for a uniformly loaded surface patch with a meaningful area. Explain when the action is unavailable for an edge, point or curved surface without a supported pressure model. With a planar patch, show the area and total before commit, for example “2 MPa × 120 mm² = 240 N.” Normal pressure is the supported MPa launch case. For oblique vectors enter N. Validate finite positive values and areas, preserve the original entered value and unit, and normalize the vector. Store force in N at the new boundary and use `N / G` in the compatibility adapter for the legacy solver's `kg` field. If an edit changes the referenced area, recompute the force from the original pressure only after the user sees the changed area and confirms it.

Show committed arrows with readable labels and a bounded visual length so small and large arrows remain selectable. The side list names each load and displays its unit, location and direction, and offers edit/remove. Treat loads in that list as acting together. Preserve separate legacy load cases and identify them in the list and results. If all loads cancel numerically or a patch cannot be resolved, tell the user which entry to review. **Next** requires one valid hold and load. Esc cancels only the active draft.

**Load details.** Ask whether the load stays on, confirm the maximum use temperature from Environment with a compact Edit control, and ask about shaking or knocks. If Yes, distinguish continuous vibration from occasional knocks. Reuse supported dynamics checks when the user can supply inputs that model needs. Otherwise mark the static result “Shaking and knocks weren't checked” and suggest a real print test. Use the existing continuous-load allowance and heat derating as applicable. Surface a thin or disconnected load path the solver cannot resolve as an analysis limitation. Keep layer-adhesion and numerical strength settings out of this step.

## 4. Analysis depth and optimizer

### Screen and job

Summarize the printer, goal and allowed changes above three compact depth cards. **Fast analysis** compares a broad set of poses cheaply and checks a small set of settings. **Extended** searches more materials, nozzles, layer heights and shell options within the same policy. **Complete** tests local changes in addition to Extended and is offered to structural goals where the relevant changes are allowed. Explain a disabled Complete offer in one line. If only Design is allowed, distinguish fixed-recipe analysis and design trials in the card copy. If a single modifier is available and the rest are still being validated, spell out what Complete can currently test. Give Quality Fast and Extended only.

Default to Extended. Show expected time as a range after collecting enough evidence from geometry count, part bounds and expected layers, candidate and load-case counts, voxel grid size, and measured worker speeds. Show “Several minutes” or “Up to the job limit” when the range is uncertain. The owner accepts a longer analysis for better results, including Complete runs around twenty minutes. Measure budgets on common and large parts before giving precise ranges. **Run analysis** starts one job. Stages read “Scoring print positions,” “Trying materials and settings,” “Checking the best options,” and, as available, “Trying local changes.” Cancel restores the last complete comparison. When a time budget expires, label checked results “Search ended early” and offer Continue or try a different depth. An exhausted or interrupted search never implies impossibility.

### Search policy

Use a baseline recipe from the selected printer, nozzle/material locks and compatible profile snapshot. Resolve requirements before ranking. Filter or flag incompatibilities involving build volume and bed stability, printer/nozzle/enclosure and flow limits, feature resolution, known material exclusions, and the selected change scope. Keep unknown compatibility visible. Check part dimensions and stable footprint after placement. Clear candidate IDs are reproducible from model and input revision, build direction/yaw, recipe, overrides and variant identity.

| Goal | Proposed ordinary-use floor | Ranking among qualifying candidates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong | ×5 | Highest verified estimated margin, then better bed stability and fewer serious print risks, then time and plastic as tiebreakers. |
| Balanced | ×3 | Starting weights of 40% time, 30% total plastic, 20% strength margin above the floor, 10% residual print risk. Normalize to a fixed printer/part baseline per run, and cap the extra strength reward. Validate weights on representative parts before release. |
| Fast | ×2, provisional | Shortest estimated total print time within flow, speed, bed and cooling limits. Break ties by risk and total plastic. |
| Light | ×1.5 | Lowest total plastic for one print, including supports. Break ties by print risk and time. Also show finished part grams. |
| Quality | No strength floor | Keep reliable poses and recipes, then prefer lower predicted surface, cooling and shape risk and less contact with visible surfaces. Among comparable results, prefer less support and then time. |

For sustained loads, use the higher of the goal floor and the existing sustained-load floor, currently ×3. Compare the worst checked load case. Treat floors as eligibility for “Meets target” in the search. If the finalist solver fails, a load is unresolved, or the field is below its validated grid resolution, show “Couldn't check strength.” A converged estimate below target is separately labeled. If the goal is infeasible in the checked space, show “None of the options we checked reached ×3” and the closest checked option if useful, plus the constraints the user can relax. “PLA only” with incompatible use conditions gets its own explanation. Offer explicit review before continuing with a below-target option, and retain the warning through export.

### Search stages and existing code

1. **Generate directions.** Use `orientation.candidate_directions` for axes, faces/resting planes and sampled directions. Include the as-loaded pose and any kept pose. Fast trims via cheap tests, Extended increases coverage and samples near promising poses. Use `placement.print_rotation` for bed fit and yaw. Keep separate shortlists for likely strength, low support, speed and printability so a pure printability prefilter doesn't throw away a strong pose. Remove duplicates only when pose, load score and print consequences are equivalent within measured tolerances.
2. **Build and prune recipes.** Seed from `recommend.goal_plan`, compatible material/profile presets, and the existing settings optimizer. Expand around a few promising candidates with bounded coordinate or beam search. Try walls, top and bottom thickness, supported infill amounts and patterns, layer heights within nozzle ranges, and eligible material/nozzle pairs. Respect minimum skinned thickness and dimensional fidelity. Exclude lightning infill from structural search until validated for the relevant load model. Compare nozzle alternatives using candidate line widths and use the actual slicer/profile flow limits where known. Check if larger lines erase holes, thin walls, gaps or engraving. Evaluate thick layers with their actual shell height and a calibrated material assumption. Carry unverified bonding effects as uncertainty and don't award them a strength uplift.
3. **Screen with inexpensive costs.** Use the isotropic field for directional ranking where effective geometry and settings make it a valid screening approximation. Reuse prepared load cases for the same mesh and mesh-dependent analysis grid. Cache slices by model, pose and layer height only where the inputs match. For coarse ranking use support volume proxies. On finalists compute sliced support grams via the existing `support_stats` path with appropriate density and support assumptions. For the same material, grams = support envelope mm³ × assumed fill × density g/cm³ ÷ 1000. Carry part grams, support grams, total grams and estimated total time separately. Time must account for support and minimum layer time best effort. If support mass cannot be estimated, mark it unknown and avoid ranking it as zero. Disclose the target support assumptions in More.
4. **Verify and compare.** Run `strength.evaluate_cases` with anisotropic properties, the actual pose, profile and selected settings on structural finalists. Run relevant slice/printability checks and take forward their measured cost values. Quality does the printability checks only. Remove dominated alternatives considering the displayed metrics, then return up to four with meaningful distinctions. Choose one first automatically, with a visible reason. Expose checked scope, excluded combinations and whether the search budget ended early. Reuse fixed-pose optimization and material screening as building blocks while keeping their public entry points and existing callers.
5. **Test local changes when allowed.** Start Complete with a bounded trial of local modifiers where slicer settings are allowed and supported. When design changes are allowed, try material additions such as a rib or local thickening, and, after slice validation, small internal voids that can create walls. Seed from stress zones and generate a short list with a maximum added volume and protected surfaces. Run browser mesh operations through `geometry.js` and use `strength_variant` as a starting point for validation. Check mesh integrity, edit conflicts, fit and load remapping, slice for the intended perimeter effect, and solve again before claiming improvement. Validate modifier export for the target slicer. Show a switchable overlay and the resulting numbers. Keep the original model as the committed version until the user chooses and validates a design variant.

Expose a new optimizer operation through the API's dispatch contract with normalized inputs and JSON-friendly output. Keep engine policy in Python and use the browser to orchestrate JS/WASM geometry work. A local runner may use a native geometry adapter with the same inputs and outputs. Avoid calling a noninterruptible long search as one opaque UI request. Use progress stages or bounded batches with worker restart fallback and preserve committed state across failures.

## 7. Problems and fixes

Use stable identities tied to the rule and tracked feature/region where possible. Findings about the whole print appear once.

The expanded row shows **Fixes** immediately under its consequence, with Slicer, Design or Process labels. **Preview** and **Add change** where the app can do it, **Show steps** for a manual process fix. Put the technical explanation and sources in **More**. Show what changes and where, for example “Round the hole top” or “Add support under this area.” Keep design-only issues visible for any scope. Disabled or incompatible fixes explain why. When the user chooses a design change outside the allowed scope, show “This changes your model. You can undo it.” Require an explicit **Add model change** for that edit. For a slicer fix outside a Design-only recipe policy, ask permission to change the recipe for that edit.

**Pending changes.** Show geometry deltas as added and removed overlays or as a valid composed preview after lightweight mesh work. Show an edited setting's new value in the pending list with the old value. Do no full numerical rechecks on Add. Give Undo, Remove and Discard all. If adding a second fix overlaps a feature or changes the same setting inconsistently, offer replacement or ask the user to remove one. Surface preview failures where they occur. Freeze bulk acceptance until conflicts resolve. Provide a persistent footer such as “3 pending changes” with **Confirm all changes**. Put **Review changes** in the same area when the list is collapsed. Navigating back preserves pending edits if their baseline is valid. Replacing the part or recipe offers to discard them or review their targets again.

**Confirmation and recheck.** Create an isolated candidate model/recipe from the last committed version. Apply edits in a repeatable order or enforce dependencies. Check mesh integrity, fit, references, support and key print constraints. If a hold/load cannot be matched confidently, show the ambiguous reference on the part and let the user reselect it before proceeding. Run the current structural margin at the chosen orientation and latest settings for structural goals. Rerun affected print checks and whichever simulations the change invalidated. A full orientation optimization is a separate user action. A small pose sanity check may flag a meaningful concern without turning the part. Process-only acknowledgments leave metrics as they were.

On success, commit a new revision and show resolved and new findings in the same Problems step. If the candidate creates a new issue that the user needs to decide about, show the candidate alongside the prior committed version with **Keep changes**, **Revise batch** or **Undo**. Below-floor results require a labeled review before acceptance. On cancel, boolean failure, missing data or solver error, preserve the valid model and pending batch and offer Retry or Edit changes. No partially confirmed batch becomes an export default. If the user skips an affected simulation, mark it stale until rerun. Acknowledging remaining findings keeps them visible in the export summary and only carries to the next revision for the same unchanged issue. Empty state reads “No problems found in the checks that ran” and names anything skipped. **Finish** moves to Done with the checked or acknowledged state.

## 8. Done, export and optional extras

**Done.** Show the chosen part on its print bed and a compact summary of material/nozzle, estimated time, part and support weight, strength if checked, and unresolved findings. Make **Export 3MF** primary. Offer changed STL and STEP where supported and where the geometry write-back succeeds. If STEP output cannot reproduce an edit, offer STL and explain the difference. Include project save and a report through the Project menu.

**Export at any point after import.** The header Export 3MF button opens a small summary of exactly what will be exported with a target slicer. Before choosing options, label it “Current part · no optimized settings yet” and export the source or an explicit baseline. With pending or stale changes, default to the last committed snapshot and identify what remains unchecked. Full export uses the chosen pose and mapped per-part settings. Show machine/profile/nozzle steps the user needs to take in their slicer and any settings the target won't honor. Validate supported OrcaSlicer, Bambu Studio and PrusaSlicer paths individually. Offer geometry-only 3MF where the settings mapping is unsupported. Below-target checks or unverified machine compatibility travel with the export summary.

**Tolerances and fits.** Open a spatial step from Done for all goals. Hover holes, pegs and slots, click to place a translucent mating pin or socket. Start with **Press**, **Snug** and **Loose** as understandable choices with a measured dimension underneath. Make partner size optional until a meaningful prediction is requested. In the preview, animate the pin sliding in, provide a Play button and show a static sectional view with a labeled clearance or interference. Define “diameter clearance” versus “gap on each side” explicitly. A print-vs-model overlay with a labeled magnification shows calibration effects when the user has measured settings. Extend to a flat gauge sliding through a slot and, later, a second imported mate as a reference only. If a feature is uncertain, ask the user to confirm or select its faces. When calibration is missing, label the clearance a preset estimate and offer the fit test. A slicer's global XY hole compensation can affect every relevant hole. Show all affected areas and stage it as a setting fix. Per-feature resizing is a design fix, follows scope override rules, and uses the same confirm-and-recheck path.

**Make it lighter.** Offer for all structural goals. Show a weight target or “Find a lighter option” with the current goal floor locked by default. Search shell/infill and local modifiers where permitted. Compare finished grams, support grams and strength with the chosen plan, and keep mount faces and fit features protected. Use standard comparison and recheck. For shape removal or internal cavities, use the Complete variant validation and preview. An unverified stress-map/topology result can be an idea to take to CAD, with no claimed saved weight on a printable model until validated.

**Tuned settings and G-code.** With a validated thermal path, show a before/after summary for time, hot spots, bonding and the controls that changed. Favor an export of tested slicer settings or supported layer ranges. Show process recommendations as instructions when the chosen slicer cannot encode them. Put any temperature adjustment in a recipe for reslicing. Make **Optimize my G-code directly** opt-in and only on supported input after T-477, T-480 and T-481. Keep original bytes downloadable. Preserve paths, bounds and extrusion, verify flow and speed constraints, firmware-specific fan commands and protected slowdowns, and rerun the thermal check. If validation fails, offer the prior 3MF path and explain the failed check. Locally generated G-code uses the compatible local OrcaSlicer path. Browser-generated G-code appears after T-127/T-483. Require a resolved printer/profile match before offering a print-ready G-code download.

## 9. Quality route

**Part → Setup → Environment → Analysis → Options → Print simulation → Problems → Done.** Selecting Quality bypasses Holds, Loads, Load details and every load-related solve, safety result or floor. Keep environment-driven material screening and bed/placeability checks. Seed with two walls and 10% infill where model and profile permit. Increase structure if a printable top or wall needs it. Rank bridges, supported overhangs, reliable bed contact, seam visibility, supported surface marks, layer steps and shape/cooling risk using only measured or supported estimates. Start visible-surface assumptions from outer exposed faces. Optionally let the user select a show face from a Quality-only 3D control after the initial comparison, then rerun with “fewer support marks here.” When calibration evidence is missing, use the printer's compatible baseline and explain uncertainty.

Research outer wall speed, cooling limits, bridge speed, layer height, seams and ironing in the target slicers. Convert tested findings into sources, settings rules and calibration cards. Keep common fixes and optional localized cosmetic changes under Problems, with the same preview, consent and confirmation. Show Tolerances and validated tuned settings as extras. Hide Lighter and Complete structural design trials.

## 10. Settings & calibration

Full-page desktop hub with a 232 px navigation column and content up to 880 px wide. Show **Back to part** when opened from a project and **Back to start** otherwise. Tabs are Printers, Materials, Print profiles and Calibration. In each view one primary action opens an inline editor or named dialog. Show which configuration the current run uses. Keep configuration changes auditable with source labels such as Built in, Imported from PrusaSlicer, and Measured on this printer.

- **Printers.** Preset search, including “My printer isn't listed,” custom printer editing and compatible nozzle/enclosure details. Surface which nozzle is installed and which sizes may be fitted. Retain known print limits such as bed dimensions and speeds. Empty state offers a searchable preset or adding a supported custom profile.
- **Materials.** Built-in material choices, custom filament profiles, optional an “I have this” marker, and calibrated values. Stock, price and spool remaining are optional and can live behind Details. Put a separate “Only my materials” control in optimizer constraints, with a helpful empty state when no materials are marked. Filter stocked materials by the same environment and device rules.
- **Print profiles.** Drag in a supported profile or select a saved one. Preview recognized values, missing required settings, unsupported entries and conflicts before saving. Associate with printer/nozzle/material as appropriate. Preserve the imported baseline on invalid input. Explicit job locks win over profile defaults. Compatible measurements override matching baseline parameters and never expand machine limits. Retain the original imported profile where existing storage supports it.
- **Calibration.** Cards for Fits, Bridges, Overhangs, Warping and Layer strength use existing printable models first. Pick printer, nozzle, filament and profile before starting. Each page has a one-line purpose, **Download model**, a short labeled diagram, slicing/printing instructions, and **Enter result**. Select the best numbered sample from a picture or keyboard list and enter a measurement only when needed. Preview the value that will change before Save. Add temperature towers, ironing and surface tests with validated instructions and settings mappings. Add sag and snap tests after T-482. A small “Used in this project” summary shows exact matches, imported defaults and missing measurements. Invalid ranges get inline errors. Handle blocked or full storage clearly, with project save as a way to carry active settings.

Persist local presets and calibration with `mypresets.js`, keyed to the relevant machine, nozzle, filament and conditions. Extend browser and Python project readers together to retain enough of the active profile and calibration snapshot to reproduce results. Continue to load older saved projects. Restored loads or fits with missing references prompt review. Opening Settings and returning preserves the active step and model. Changing calibration marks just the outputs that depend on it stale.

## Open questions for the owner

1. Are ×5 Strong, ×3 Balanced, ×2 Fast and ×1.5 Light the floors you want to ship? Should any below-target 3MF export require more than a clear acknowledgment and labeling?
2. When you choose **Design**, should a confirmed baseline also lock orientation, or may the app turn the part while keeping its material and slicer settings fixed? This plan treats pose as a baseline choice and keeps it fixed during design search.
3. For **Quality**, should the app ever ask which face people will see before the first comparison, or is it enough to let someone mark it during comparison when the default result looks wrong?
4. Which current standalone tools must remain available on day one outside this flow, especially electronics-box heat and the versions/report utilities? The launch scope can retain a separate entry if those are daily workflows.
5. Which printer, material and parts can you use for the first physical tests? A common machine and a real bracket plus the planned thermal fixtures would help decide both recommendation quality and the thermal go/no-go.
