# T-342: fillets and stress concentration in printed polymers

Research note, 23 November 2026. Scope: static tensile and fatigue evidence for FDM fillets and notches; this is not a calibration. Values below are attributed and distinguish measured results from the application's settings.

## Current behaviour

* `rules.yaml`, `stress_at_inside_corner`, recommends a 1–3 mm inside fillet (larger for large parts), a 45° chamfer where a round would overhang, five walls, and turning the part so the load does not pull layers apart. Its sources are Printed Solid's design article, Zabihollah et al. below, a 2025 ETASR shoulder-geometry FEA, and Rahix's print-direction article. These support general advice, not a measured universal 1–3 mm optimum. `thresholds.yaml` sets the separate finding-based `foot_radius_mm` to 1.0 mm, citing Printed Solid, Rahix and ETASR.
* `features.py` finds the connected inside corner closest to a strength hot spot and other hot corners. The default `stress_chamfer_mm` is 2.0 mm, adjustable 0.5–8.0 mm. It uses a 45° chamfer if the round would exceed the material's overhang limit; otherwise the radius is multiplied by sqrt(0.5/(1−π/4)) ≈ 1.526, giving a default round of about 3.1 mm. This is equal cross-sectional added area to the 2 mm chamfer, not a measured Kt correction. The separate foot-fillet path uses 1 mm without that scaling. T-283 records variants of 1/2/3/4 mm, or 2/4 mm for the quick choice; see implementation before assuming these are fillet radii rather than the size parameter.
* `strength.py` uses anisotropic voxel FEA and element stresses/failure indices, including build-direction weakness and optional sliced line/bond information. It does not apply an explicit notch Kt, Kf or q. The default mesh target is 12,000 voxels, and two element rings at loads/supports are excluded to mitigate singularities. Therefore the model resolves added material only as far as its grid permits; a sharp corner peak is mesh dependent. A fillet smaller than a few voxels cannot be assumed to have its real local peak resolved. T-282 reports the team's own mushroom comparison and explicitly observes that its approximately 1 mm cubes see added plastic rather than the smoothness advantage; this is an internal numerical observation, not printed-part validation.

## Target paper — read by hand

Abolghassem Zabihollah, Stephen Bardowell, Bryan Does and Rajesh Vuddandam (2024), “Experimental Determination of Tensile Stress Concentration Factors of 3D-Printed PLA Polymers: Fillet Radius, Hole Diameter, and Infill Density,” *Advanced Materials Research* 1180, 13–22, https://doi.org/10.4028/p-9hyd0a; publisher https://www.scientific.net/AMR.1180.13.

Crossref's publisher-supplied abstract reports tensile tests on standard PLA specimens with different fillet radii, in-plane hole diameters and infill densities, and says stress concentration coefficients were determined as functions of these variables. It gives no numerical results, geometry ratios, raster or build orientation. On 23 November 2026 the publisher PDF URL `https://www.scientific.net/AMR.1180.13.pdf` returned HTTP 403 (Cloudflare challenge); OpenAlex classified the paper closed and listed no repository full text. Thus **read the full paper by hand** before extracting tables/graphs. Measured Kt, Peterson/theoretical Kt, radius, width ratios, density levels, raster, build orientation, replicates and scatter are all **unknown from the accessible abstract**. Do not attribute a number or a Peterson comparison to it until checked. The abstract mentions geometry, but does not establish that it uses Peterson for the comparison. Reliability: primary experiment exists; abstract-only evidence cannot calibrate a numerical model.

## Other evidence and limits

* Mohammed Algarni (2022), “Fatigue Behavior of PLA Material and the Effects of Mean Stress and Notch: Experiments and Modeling,” *Procedia Structural Integrity* 37, 676–683, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prostr.2022.01.137. The OpenAlex abstract reports 180 load-controlled tests, R = −1, 0.1 and 0.3, and three rectangular specimen notch categories (large, medium, sharp). It reports significant effects of notches and mean stress on high-cycle fatigue life. The ScienceDirect PDF request returned 403 in this session; the abstract does not specify notch radii, raster, infill, numerical Kf or q. Reliability: primary fatigue experiment, abstract-only here; do not transfer its load ratios or qualitative conclusion into a Kf table.
* P. Seibert, L. Susmel, F. Berto, M. Kästner and N. Razavi (2021), “Applicability of strain energy density criterion for fracture prediction of notched PLA specimens produced via fused deposition modeling,” *Engineering Fracture Mechanics*, article 108103, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engfracmech.2021.108103. Its accessible OpenAlex abstract reports experimental notched FDM PLA fracture data and compares averaged strain energy density with the theory of critical distances; it warns that estimating the control radius from measured fracture toughness was suboptimal for this complex material. No q, notch dimensions, raster or numerical reduction were verified here. Reliability: primary study, abstract-only here; useful caution that a simple point-stress correction needs validation.
* M. Azadi, A. Dadashi, S. Dezianian, M. Kianifar, S. Torkaman and M. Chiyani (2021), “High-cycle bending fatigue properties of additive-manufactured ABS and PLA polymers fabricated by fused deposition modeling 3D-printing,” *Forces in Mechanics*, article 100016, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmec.2021.100016. Relevant fatigue background for ABS and PLA; notch factors and comparable geometry were not verified, so it is not evidence for an ABS/PLA q value or a bracket fillet.
* Jorge Guillermo Diaz Rodriguez, Alberto David Pertuz Comas and Juan David Sandoval Herrera (2023), “Notch Sensitivity Study in U-notched Polymers Built by Additive Manufacturing (AM),” *Frattura ed Integrità Strutturale*, https://doi.org/10.3221/igf-esis.66.07. Publisher abstract describes PA6/short-carbon-fibre Onyx, axial fatigue R = 0.1 and U-notch radii 0.25–2 mm, comparing static and cyclic concentration. The PDF was obtainable, but numerical tables and print conditions were not checked. This is **not** PLA, PETG or ABS; it shows a possible test approach, not transferable coefficients.

No reliable numerical PLA/PETG/ABS Kf or q by layer direction, or measured L-bracket/shoulder radius versus strength, was established in this search. In particular, a notched impact energy is not a tensile or fatigue Kf, and fatigue Kf depends on life, load ratio, geometry and reference specimen. Infill and contour toolpaths around a notch may change when the radius changes; orientation labels alone are insufficient. Do not infer a quantitative across-layer/along-layer correction from these abstracts.

## Recommendation

1. Retain the present 1–3 mm as a **practical starting suggestion**, not a measured optimum or guarantee; retain printability selection and the option to compare variants. No evidence here supports a numerical diminishing-returns radius or a universal r/d target. Report available size/clearance, print orientation and the solver's approximate nature. Compare multiple feasible radii and stop when additional gain in the *computed* safety factor is small, clearly labelled a numerical design comparison, not an experimentally established optimum. Verify that each change spans enough voxels and re-run at finer resolution; a stable ranking alone does not validate absolute stress or fatigue life.
2. For future calibration, collect matched smooth and notched printed specimens for each material, geometry, orientation and print recipe, recording radius r, narrow width d, shoulder width D, thickness, r/d and D/d, raster, walls, infill and measured cross section. Separate static strength from fatigue at specified life and R, and report replication and uncertainty. Extract the target paper's full tables first. Avoid interpolation outside measured ranges or transferring flat tensile coupons to L-brackets without validation.
3. If a suitable nominal-stress model and matched measurements become available, evaluate `Kf = 1 + q (Kt_theory − 1)` with `Kf` defined as smooth/notched nominal failure stress at the same fatigue life (or explicitly defined static strength ratio), and derive `q = (Kf − 1)/(Kt_theory − 1)` for each matched case. Kt must match the geometry, loading and nominal-stress convention. Do not assume q is a material constant across orientation, geometry and fatigue life, and do not insert arbitrary q values. A measured table versus r/d also needs D/d and the other conditions; one radius ratio alone is insufficient.
4. Do not multiply the existing voxel peak by theoretical Kt or Kf: it already reflects some concentration and geometry, so that would double-count an unknown fraction. A future correction would require a documented nominal-stress baseline or a mesh-dependent calibration against physical specimens and convergence studies, and must account for anisotropic failure and layer/toolpath changes. Keep calibration separate from the geometry sizing heuristic.

Confidence: high in the described code paths and the absence of verified numerical calibration; moderate in the qualitative experimental conclusions quoted from abstracts; low in predicting real printed-part strength or an optimal radius without full papers and matched tests.
