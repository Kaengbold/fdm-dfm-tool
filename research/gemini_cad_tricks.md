# CAD Tricks for FDM (T-181)

| trick | problem it solves | when to use it | typical numbers (sizes, angles) | source URL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Print-in-place and movable part clearances | Fused hinges, pins, or sliding joints due to extrusion width variation and thermal expansion. | Designing hinges, snap fits, or mating pins and holes. | At least 0.3 mm radial/axial clearance for movable parts; test range 0.1 mm to 0.5 mm. | https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135 |
| Bottom elephant-foot chamfer | First layer squished against the build plate flares outward, ruining dimensional fit of the bottom edge. | Parts printed flat on the bed that must slot into another component or sit flush without deburring. | 0.1 mm to 0.4 mm bottom chamfer (or compensated via slicer Elephant Foot Compensation). | https://www.printables.com/model/1418015-tolerance-test |
| Orienting load-bearing hooks/brackets horizontally on the bed | Delamination along Z-layer lines under tensile or bending loads. | Structural hooks, brackets, and mechanical parts subjected to pulling or bending forces. | Horizontal orientation bore 35 kg vs 6 kg standing vertically in ABS hook tests (2 perimeters, 30% cubic infill). | https://www.cnckitchen.com/blog/can-acetone-glue-layers-of-3d-prints-together |
| Staggered / "Brick" perimeter layers | Weak interlaminar fracture planes where adjacent perimeter extrusions align on the exact same Z height. | Structural parts requiring higher Z-strength and resistance to crack propagation between perimeters. | Tested at 0.25 mm layer height with 4 perimeters and staggered Z offsets. | https://www.cnckitchen.com/blog/brick-layers-make-3d-prints-stronger |
| Designing overhangs within self-supporting angles | Drooping or curling perimeters when printing over air without support material. | Undersides of brackets, chamfers, and cutouts to eliminate support material. | Keep overhangs below 45°–50° from vertical; 50°–75° requires tuned cooling and reduced layer height. | https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135 |
