          Cooling: does each layer cool in time?

          Finds spots that can't lose their heat fast enough: a thin tip or pin, or a small area printed on top of a big block of hot plastic. If the layer below is still soft when the next one goes on, it droops, blobs or bends. Counts the part fan and the slicer slowing down small layers.

          Will it hold?

          Tap a face on the part. Then say if it's held there (screwed, glued, clamped) or loaded (a weight hangs on it, something pushes or pulls). You need one of each.

          Loads in the same case act together. Use separate cases for different situations (hanging, being knocked sideways): the weakest one counts.

              Plastic slowly stretches and sags under a load that never goes away (creep), so the check then asks for ×3 safety instead of ×2.

              How it's printed:

              Most strength comes from the solid outside: walls around the sides and solid layers on top and bottom. Inside is a light infill grid. These go into the 3MF too.

              Optional simulations

              Extra checks that take longer. None is needed: run the ones that matter for your part.

              How warm or cold it gets

              Plastic gets weaker as it warms up and soft near its limit. The strength check and the other checks use this temperature.

              Warping: will the corners lift?

              Simulates the print layer by layer: the upper layers shrink as they cool and pull the corners off the bed.

              Shaking and knocks

              Uses where it is held and the load from Will it hold?: does it start to swing when something mounted on it shakes, and does it survive the load dropping onto it?

              Set where it is held and a load in Will it hold? first.

              Heat inside an electronics box

              If this part is a closed box around electronics, everything they use turns into heat that has to get out through the walls. Quick estimate from the box's outside surface.

              A Raspberry Pi 4 uses about 3–6 W, an ESP32 or Arduino under 1 W. Vents help: the result lies between the two choices.

                Slots and flat gaps

                Something that slides into a slot, or a tab that goes into one: click the two flat faces on either side, then say how thick the other part is and how tight.                   Hole compensation in the slicer

                  Small holes print a little too small. The slicer can make every hole bigger by a set amount: use it when you can't change the model. For one exact fit, sizing the hole above is better.

                  Tune it to your printer

                  Print the fit test once (a 6 mm peg and holes 6.0 … 6.5 mm) and say which hole fits how. Every size here then uses your printer's real tolerance instead of a typical one. More test prints are in the same place.

                      A lighter shape

                      Keeps the plastic only where your load runs through the part. It's blocky and not a finished part: use it as a guide to redesign the part in CAD. Needs the loads from "Will it hold?".

                      Your versions

                      Each time you apply changes, a new version is kept. Switch between them on the 3D view (or with Ctrl+Z) and compare.

                      Advanced design changes

                      Boxes you place on the part for the slicer: supports where you want them, none where you don't, and hidden gaps that make it print solid walls inside. They go into the 3MF; the strength check counts the gaps.

                        Add another model

                        Put a second part onto this one and print them as one: a bracket onto a box, a knob onto a lid. Its biggest flat face goes flat onto the face you click, centred where you click. It becomes a new version of the model (Undo goes back).

                        Stronger in one area

                        More walls and infill only where it's needed: around a screw hole, a hinge, a clip. The rest stays light. The area goes into the 3MF as a modifier (orange box) and counts in the strength check. Let it reach well past the spot that carries the load: where a stiff area ends, the stress gathers.
