          Cooling: does each layer cool in time?

          Finds spots that can't lose their heat fast enough: a thin tip or pin, or a small area printed on top of a big block of hot plastic. If the layer below is still soft when the next one goes on, it droops, blobs or bends. Counts the part fan and the slicer slowing down small layers.

          Will it hold?

          Tap a face on the part. Then say if it's held there (screwed, glued, clamped) or loaded (a weight hangs on it, something pushes or pulls). You need one of each.

          Loads in the same case act together. Use separate cases for different situations (hanging, being knocked sideways): the weakest one counts.

              Plastic slowly stretches and sags under a load that never goes away (creep), so the check then asks for ×3 safety instead of ×2.

              How it's printed:

              Most strength comes from the solid outside: walls around the sides and solid layers on top and bottom. Inside is a light infill grid. These go into the 3MF too.

              Optional simulations

              Extra checks that take longer. None is needed: run the ones that matter for your part.

              Warping: will the corners lift?

              Simulates the print layer by layer: the upper layers shrink as they cool and pull the corners off the bed.

              Shaking and knocks

              Uses where it is held and the load from Will it hold?: does it start to swing when something mounted on it shakes, and does it survive the load dropping onto it?
